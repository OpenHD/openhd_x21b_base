# X21B IMX662 camera port

This port targets a bare IMX662 module with **four CSI lanes and an onboard
24 MHz crystal**, using the X21B's CSI0 receiver and I2C5 at address `0x1a`.
The kernel driver defaults to 1920x1080 RAW10, linear/non-HDR, at its calculated maximum
window-mode rate. The Rockchip ISP processes the sensor's Bayer output.

## Resolution and frame rate

| Output | Sensor window | Depth | HMAX | Minimum VMAX | Calculated ceiling | CSI link |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1920x1080 | 1920x1080 | RAW10 | 660 | 1150 | 97.826 fps | 360 MHz |
| 1280x720 | 1280x752 | RAW10 | 660 | 822 | 136.861 fps | 360 MHz |
| 1920x1080 | 1920x1080 | RAW12 | 990 | 1150 | 65.217 fps | 297 MHz |
| 1280x720 | 1280x752 | RAW12 | 990 | 822 | 91.241 fps | 297 MHz |
| 1920x1080 full frame | 1936x1100 all-pixel | RAW10 | 660 | 1250 | 90 fps | 360 MHz |
| 1920x1080 full frame | 1936x1100 all-pixel | RAW12 | 990 | 1250 | 60 fps | 297 MHz |
| 1920x1080 DOL2 HDR | 1936x1100 all-pixel, two VCs | RAW10 | 660 | FSC 2500 / VMAX 1250 | 45 fps | 360 MHz |

These are **sensor timing calculations, not measured X21 throughput**:
`fps = 74250000 / (HMAX * VMAX)`. Sony's window-mode constraints require
height at least 752, VMAX at least height plus 70 and at least 820, and an
even VMAX. The published full-scan 90/60 fps modes use VMAX 1250; the higher
rates here are inferred from the shorter permitted windows. Four lanes carry
720 Mbit/s per lane in RAW10 and 594 Mbit/s per lane in RAW12.

The 720p mode uses a centered 1280x752 sensor crop, then the ISP removes
16 lines at each edge to output exactly 1280x720. It has a tighter field of
view. The driver advertises 1280x752, because native 1280x720 violates the
sensor's minimum window height. No binning or full-view scaling is used.

After stopping camera capture and the encoder, use the installed helper:

```sh
x21-imx662-mode 1080p max
x21-imx662-mode 720p max
x21-imx662-mode full90 max
x21-imx662-mode hdr45 max
# Optional lower integer rate or RAW12:
x21-imx662-mode 720p 120
x21-imx662-mode 1080p max raw12
```

The helper discovers the sensor and ISP media graphs, sets the sensor format
and interval, configures the ISP crop, and selects NV12 on `rkisp_mainpath`.
It prints the negotiated formats. Interval requests are rounded to even VMAX
and clamped to sensor limits; read back the actual interval. The OpenHD/ArtLink
capture setup must preserve these settings rather than overwrite them during
startup. Its resolution/FPS selection has not been integrated or tested here.
Sustained ISP, memory, RKAIQ and encoder throughput still needs board testing.

## Build

The source lives directly in the native X21 repositories:

- `OpenHD/openhd_x21b_kernel`: driver, Kconfig/Makefile, built-in kernel config,
  device tree and timing/exposure tests.
- `OpenHD/openhd_x21b_base` (this repository): board defconfig, rootfs IQ tuning,
  executable mode helper and helper command tests.
- `OpenHD/openhd_x21b_manifest`: `imx662.xml` pins the matching kernel/base commits.

Select `camera: imx662` in OpenHD-X21B's workflow. It initializes the pinned
manifest and builds `rv1126b:openhd_x21b_imx662_defconfig`. No source installer
or patch-copy step is involved. The original board defconfig and devBoard1
remain separate. For a synchronized native SDK:

```sh
./build.sh rv1126b:openhd_x21b_imx662_defconfig
./build.sh all
```

The dedicated camera graph replaces six original candidates on this connector
and the native kernel defconfig enables `CONFIG_VIDEO_IMX662=y`.

## Full-frame and HDR

`full90` selects `WINMODE=0`, HMAX 660 and VMAX 1250: the documented 90 fps
all-pixel RAW10 mode. The sensor includes processing margins in its 1936x1100
readout; the ISP selects the 1920x1080 recording region at (8,8).

`hdr45` selects the workbook's fastest full-frame DOL2 VC mode: WDMODE 1,
THIN_V_EN 1, two RAW10 exposures on VC0/VC1, HMAX 660 and sensor VMAX 1250.
Rockchip controls and frame intervals use FSC = 2 * VMAX (2500), giving 45 fps.
The driver implements `RKMODULE_SET/GET_HDR_CFG`, HDR mode enumeration,
channel mapping, exposure readback and `PREISP_CMD_SET_HDRAE_EXP`. Mode changes
are rejected during streaming. A stopped-stream `hdr_sensor_mode` control
allows the helper to select HDR. RKAIQ must also start in HDR2 mode and the
ISP/CIF pipeline must capture and merge both VCs; changing a sensor control
alone does not enable ISP merging. This full HDR path is not hardware-tested.

RHS1 stays at the workbook's 231 lines. Long exposure is even and clamped to
8..FSC-250 lines; short exposure is even and clamped to 2..226 lines. SHR0
therefore stays even and at least 250, SHR1 stays odd and at least 5, and the
readout boundaries do not move between frames. Separate gains are capped at
24 dB. Linear exposure/gain/HCG controls are inactive in HDR. IQ HDR2 limits
match these conservative constraints; other ISP curves remain imported.

Clear HDR is distinct from DOL2 and is not exposed by this driver. Sony's
manual permits cropped Clear HDR. Assuming its two readouts can use the
window minimum of 822 lines each, a 1280x752 crop would give
`74250000 / (660 * 2 * 822) = 68.43 fps`, followed by a 720-line ISP crop.
That is a conditional timing estimate, not a validated mode or guaranteed
X21 rate. The supplied normal-mode manual/workbook lack the full Clear HDR
application-note constraints and gain/merge calibration needed to claim it.
Do not apply the linear 136.86 fps timing directly to Clear HDR.

## Tuning

The default IQ JSON and the `imx662-scenes/{moon,planet,sun}.json` profiles originate from
the supplied Seestar RV1126B S30Plus ISP35 bundle. `docs/cameras/imx662-sources.json`
records original paths and SHA-256 checksums. The driver is based on the
[will127534 IMX662 driver](https://github.com/will127534/imx662-v4l2-driver),
commit `e9e3ec4b9dbd8c38368ca27831cb0bca6ba07cbc`, adapted through the
earlier Rockchip 6.1 port, cross-checked against
[BellssGit driver and overlay](https://github.com/BellssGit/IMX662_module_for_raspberry_pi)
at `9871e23f1a9376c3fa9b4c234ee62ea72197c96b`. The ADC setup values
`0x3a50..0x3a52` were checked against the supplied register workbook for both
bit depths. The four-lane data rates also appear in the
[IMX662 driver submission](https://www.spinics.net/lists/devicetree/msg904399.html). Its original copyright and GPL notice are retained.

The normal tuning is installed by the X21 rootfs overlay as
`/etc/iqfiles/imx662_CMK-OT1234-FV0_M00-2MP-F00.json` and
`/etc/iqfiles/imx662_default_default.json`. The module/lens strings in DT
match the former filename so RKAIQ can discover it. Alternative astronomical
profiles are stored under `/etc/iqfiles/imx662-scenes/`; normal is the default.
The default alias is a symlink to the canonical JSON.

VMAX controls accept only even line counts. Exposure remains in single-line
units. The Sony reference clock for HMAX timing is 74.25 MHz, independent of
the selected input clock. The local register workbook and software manual were
used to verify these constraints; they are not copied into this repository.

Sensor calibration changes relative to the extracted files:

- Gain register units are 0.3 dB, matching IMX662's native gain control.
- Linear exposure spans 2 through VMAX minus 8 lines, matching SHR limits.
- The retained AE minimum frame rate is 30 fps; it is not an upper limit.

Automatic exposure can lengthen frames in low light. For sustained maximum
FPS, verify RKAIQ's frame-rate and exposure configuration on the board and
keep exposure within the selected frame period. Changing the sensor format
alone does not guarantee that AE or the encoder sustains its timing ceiling.

Other ISP tuning is retained. These profiles were calibrated for the Seestar
optics, so color, shading, exposure behavior, and noise reduction must be
checked with the actual X21 lens. HDR merge curves and response need board validation; the driver supports
DOL2 HDR_X2 and rejects other HDR mode requests.

## Validation and hardware bring-up

Source validation uses the X21 kernel at
`32e6c42c40dc34296b1bfdf5a573e99199ee44ac` (Linux 6.1.141):

```sh
make ARCH=arm64 CROSS_COMPILE=aarch64-linux-gnu- rockchip_linux_ohd_defconfig
make ARCH=arm64 CROSS_COMPILE=aarch64-linux-gnu- -j8 \
    drivers/media/i2c/imx662.o rockchip/rv1126bp-ohd-x21b-imx662.dtb
make ARCH=arm64 CROSS_COMPILE=aarch64-linux-gnu- -j8 Image
```

Validated locally: driver object, IMX662 DTB, and full `Image` builds passed.
The linked `vmlinux` contains the IMX662 driver. The compiled DTB has I2C5
address `0x1a`, four lanes, 297/360 MHz links, a fixed 24 MHz clock, reciprocal
endpoints, and no competing camera nodes. Workflow YAML/shell syntax and IQ JSON parsing passed. Imported ISP curves
are retained, with sensor exposure/gain metadata adapted to this driver. Host tests exercise the
actual driver mode tables and interval functions, including overflow inputs,
crop constraints and both bit depths. Mock media tools verify the helper's
sensor-to-ISP resolution mapping. The window-enable register and ADC settings
were checked against the supplied sensor references. Run those checks with:

```sh
python3 kernel-6.1/tools/testing/imx662/test_modes.py
python3 tests/cameras/test_imx662_helper.py
```

A full firmware/rootfs build and physical camera tests have not been run for
this change. The helper's commands have not been validated on a live media graph.

No reset GPIO is assumed: the existing X21 camera graph provides no module
reset assignment. The module must supply its rails and release XCLR itself,
or the verified reset/supply connections must be added to DT. The fixed clock
describes the module crystal; it does not drive the X21 camera-clock pin.

After booting an IMX662 image, verify the probe with `dmesg`, inspect the media
graph with `media-ctl -p`, and confirm an `m00_f_imx662` subdevice with RAW10
1920x1080 and four lanes. Probe currently verifies register accessibility,
not a unique sensor ID. Then check RKAIQ loads the installed IQ file, capture
frames through the ISP at both resolutions, and measure sustained FPS,
exposure/gain changes, Bayer colors and OpenHD encoder output. Check crop
calibration, especially lens shading: the imported IQ resolution is 1920x1080
and was calibrated for different optics. A compiled driver/kernel is not hardware capture
or image-quality validation. This port does not flash a board automatically.
